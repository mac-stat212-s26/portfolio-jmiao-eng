Explore the week’s TidyTuesday challenge. Develop a research question, then answer it through a short data story with effective visualization(s). Provide sufficient background for readers to grasp your narrative.

Repair Cafes are volunteer-run community events where people bring broken household items and try to fix them with help from experienced repairers. The dataset for this week comes from Repair Monitor and contains records of repair attempts from Repair Cafes worldwide. Since these events focus on reducing waste and extending product life, the data can help show which types of items are most likely to be repaired successfully.

Data Cleaning

Code
repair_summary <- repairs %>%
  filter(!is.na(category), !is.na(repaired)) %>%
  mutate(
    repair_success = repaired == "yes"
  ) %>%
  group_by(category) %>%
  summarise(
    total_repairs = n(),
    repaired_n = sum(repair_success, na.rm = TRUE),
    repair_rate = mean(repair_success, na.rm = TRUE)
  ) %>%
  ungroup()

top_categories <- repair_summary %>%
  filter(total_repairs >= 100) %>%
  arrange(desc(total_repairs)) %>%
  slice_head(n = 10)

The graph shows that repair success rates vary a lot across product categories. Among the 10 most common categories, Textile has the highest repair success rate at about 92.2%, making it the most successfully repaired category in this analysis. However, the most common category is Household appliances electric, with 70,983 repair attempts, and its repair success rate is only about 55.3%. This suggests that the most frequently repaired items are not always the easiest to fix. In general, categories like textiles, bicycles, and non-electric tools seem to be repaired more successfully, while equipment involving electronics appears to be more difficult to repair.
